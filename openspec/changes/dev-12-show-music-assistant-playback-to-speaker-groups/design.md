## Context

- `SessionProvider::claims` is synchronous and, by "Claims are decided
  offline", uses only configuration and the origin. The Music Assistant
  provider follows the player (`follow`) and polls `players/get` and
  `player_queues/get` only while it claims.
- BluOS reports a Music Assistant group session as `service` AirPlay,
  `serviceType` Endpoint, a `streamUrl` like `AirPlay:airplay_pcm01:…`; the
  device mapping turns it into a service origin "AirPlay", cast.
- The recordings (`~/jukelight-recordings/` on the reference host, copied
  to the Mac; not in the repository) cover: a group playing and paused, a
  phone's AirPlay, Qobuz Connect, and the subwoofer off and on.
- The kiosk's `SignalPath` (DEV-37/42/43) draws a row of waves with a
  stepped, change-keyed pulse; the mocks for this change are in
  `/tmp/speakers/` (`layouts.html`, generated by `gen2.py`), local only.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Recognise Music Assistant's group sessions without misreading a phone's
  AirPlay.
- Model the speakers and outputs in the domain's words, not Music
  Assistant's or BluOS's.
- One diagram component with two directions, which fits every scenario
  without overflowing.

**Non-Goals:**
- BluOS groups, per-speaker volume.

## Decisions

### 1. AirPlay claims from a cached answer

`claims` stays synchronous. While the device reports an AirPlay origin,
the provider polls `players/get` every 2 s (as it polls while claiming) and
keeps the latest answer with its time. `claims` returns true for an AirPlay
origin when that answer is at most 5 s old and its `active_source` is a
Music Assistant queue id: this player's key (it leads, or plays alone),
or the leader's id that `synced_to` names (it is a member). Found while
applying: this needs no `players/all` lookup. A change of the answer ticks
`updates`, so the snapshot is rebuilt at once.

- *Alternative:* an async claim. Rejected: the arbiter rebuilds snapshots
  synchronously; an async claim would ripple through the application.
- *Alternative:* claim every AirPlay session while Music Assistant runs.
  Rejected: a phone's AirPlay would show Music Assistant's last queue.

`active_source` of a group member names the leader's queue, so the content
comes from `player_queues/get` for that id.

### 2. Domain: speakers on the content, outputs on the device

`Content.speakers: Vec<Speaker>` with `Speaker { name, kind:
SpeakerKind (Speaker | Computer), playing, quality: Option<AudioQuality> }`
comes from the provider. `DeviceSnapshot.outputs: Vec<Output>` with
`Output { name: Option<String>, kind: OutputKind (Speaker | Subwoofer) }`
comes from the device (the subwoofer) and from configuration (the output
speaker), merged in the application. The quality class of a speaker uses
the existing `AudioQuality::class`.

- *Alternative:* one list of "outputs" for both. Rejected: the speakers
  are the session's (they come and go with the group), the outputs are the
  player's (they stay).

### 3. Music Assistant: speakers from the group, formats from the outputs

The provider fetches `players/all` once per group change (cached like the
providers list) to resolve the members' names, kinds and states. A member's
format comes from the `audio_processing.outputs` entry naming it. The kind
is `Computer` for Music Assistant's web player (`provider` `sendspin` whose
`device_info.manufacturer` is Music Assistant), `Speaker` otherwise.

### 4. Names in the application, from configuration

`SPEAKER_NAMES` (a JSON object) and `PLAYER_OUTPUT_NAME` are read by the
`app` crate into a `SpeakerNames` value passed to the player monitor, which
renames speakers when it builds the snapshot. An invalid value logs a
warning and is ignored (so a typo never stops the kiosk). Matching is by
Music Assistant's displayed name; the admin app (DEV-6) can later match by
id, which is easy to pick from a list but awkward to type into `.env`.

### 5. API

`speakers` and `outputs` lists; each speaker's `quality` reuses
`QualityDto` and adds `class`. `device.markUrl` reuses the logo route with
the brand `<maker>-mark`.

### 6. Kiosk: one diagram, two directions

`SignalDiagram` replaces `SignalPath`: nodes (source, Music Assistant,
speaker rows, streamer, outputs) placed by a small layout function from the
snapshot and the available box (measured with a `ResizeObserver`), drawn as
one SVG. The layout tries its options in the fixed give-way order and takes
the first that fits, measuring each (as the mock's `gen2.py` does), rather
than estimating heights. Text is fitted with the canvas's `measureText`,
not character counts. The direction is chosen by the queue: top to bottom
with a queue, otherwise left to right if every part fits in full.

- *Alternative:* CSS layout (flex/grid) with HTML nodes. Rejected: the
  lines, junctions and rounded corners need coordinates; mixing SVG lines
  with HTML boxes makes the two drift.

### 7. The pulse along a tree

The diagram's lines are split into straight segments. Each segment has a
bright band animated with `transform` along it, `steps()` at about 15 a
second, as today, keyed by the same change key (DEV-43). A segment's
`animation-delay` is the length of the path before it divided by the
pulse's speed, so each speaker's branch starts as the pulse passes its
junction on the line down to the streamer, and the outputs start when the
pulse reaches the streamer. (The rows branch off one vertical line, so a
pulse that reached them all at once would have to jump ahead of itself.) Ports light up with
`opacity` at their arrival time.

### 8. A paused group: the provider holds the session

Recordings show that when Music Assistant pauses a group, the player
reports `stop` with its own previous queue (a Qobuz track, not AirPlay),
and Music Assistant's queue for the player turns `idle` but keeps
`current_item` and `elapsed_time`. Music Assistant's stop looks the same.

The provider remembers the item of the last group session it claimed.
When the device then reports stopped, it enters a third mode, *held*: it
keeps polling `players/get` and `player_queues/get` every 2 s and claims
the device while the queue is not playing and its current item is still
that item. Its content is then the group's (track, position at the
queue's `elapsed_time`, queue, speakers) with `Content.held` set. The
arbiter reports a held content's state as paused; otherwise the state
still comes from the device. The hold ends when the device reports
anything but stopped (a resumed group is claimed again as before, any
other session takes over) or when the queue's item changes or clears.

- *Alternative:* keep the paused screen for a fixed time, then idle.
  Rejected: a pause on the player itself has no such limit; the display
  grace period already turns the screen off, the same for both.
- *Alternative:* tell a pause from a stop. Not possible: Music Assistant
  reports both as `idle` with the item kept. A stopped group shows as
  paused until the screen goes off.

### Tests per layer

- **Domain unit:** `Speaker`, `Output`, the class of a speaker's quality.
- **Music Assistant fixtures** (scrubbed copies of the recordings): each
  claim scenario, the group's queue (leader and member), the speakers and
  their formats, a dropped speaker, playing alone.
- **BluOS fixtures:** subwoofer on and off.
- **Application with fake ports:** AirPlay claim without an answer,
  outputs merged, names applied, invalid names ignored.
- **HTTP:** `speakers`, `outputs`, `markUrl`.
- **Kiosk components:** each "Signal diagram", "Streamer card" and "Line
  colours" scenario; the pulse's delays along the tree.
- **e2e, landscape and portrait:** fake scenarios for the cases in the
  mock (group, many speakers, dropped speaker, long names, AirPlay,
  Bluetooth, radio, Music Assistant unavailable, no queue); for each, an
  overflow check (nothing past the screen, under the footer, into the
  queue's column, or over a logo) and a screenshot.
- **Reference host:** the recorded situations live, and an hour's CPU.
- **Held group (layer 4):** fixture tests with the paused recording
  (held, resumed, another session, item cleared), provider tests for the
  polling while held, and an arbiter test for the paused state.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- [Music Assistant renames `active_source` or its group fields] →
  `serde` defaults: the AirPlay session stays unclaimed and shows as today.
- [A 5 s window where a phone's AirPlay right after a group shows the
  group] → the answer is refreshed every 2 s; at most one or two polls.
- [More lines, more pulse frames] → the pulse runs only on a change
  (DEV-43); all branches move in the same frames; measured on the host.
- [Names in `.env` are personal] → only example names in the repository,
  fixtures and docs.

## Migration Plan

The API only gains fields. `SPEAKER_NAMES` and `PLAYER_OUTPUT_NAME` are
optional; without them the kiosk shows reported names and no output
speaker. Rollback is a revert of the stack.
